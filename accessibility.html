Accessibility

TerraSense Jukebox is designed to be used by sound, touch, keyboard or screen reader, with no sight needed.

Display

These apply to every page and are remembered in this browser. The same switches are under Controls in the Player.

Display

The site also follows your system's dark mode and reduced-motion settings.

Keyboard

In the Player, press Tab to reach the map, then use these keys. At the edge of the map you hear a low bump and the edge is announced.

With a screen reader

  • The map is an interactive region, so your screen reader passes the arrow keys to it. Press Tab to leave it.
  • Each move is announced politely, without interrupting: the variable, the approximate value, and where you are, for example "Sea surface temperature: about 28 °C, near the equator, 3° S, 140° W."
  • The announcement uses exactly the same words as the panel on screen ("Last announcement").
  • Under "Find and compare", the Summary button reads out the warmest and coolest areas and the pattern from the equator to the poles; the Go to buttons jump to those areas; Compare tells you the difference between two points you mark. All of it is worked out from the decoded colors.
  • The first section of the Player, "Start here", comes before the map. It has the Voice switch, the Tour, the Summary, "Where am I?" and a menu to change frame.
  • The Voice switch has three choices: Screen reader (announcements go to your screen reader), Built-in voice (the browser reads aloud, for people not using a screen reader), or Off (text on screen only). Only one voice is used at a time, so the two never talk over each other.
  • Under Controls, "How much to say" has three levels: Short (value only), Normal (variable, value and place) and Full (also how far up the legend). "Where am I?" and the Go to buttons always say the place.
  • Moving the mouse plays sound but is not announced, unless you switch on "Read the value when the mouse stops moving". On a touch screen, the value is read when you lift your finger.
  • Errors (a frame that will not load, sound that will not start) are announced at once and play a short falling tone.
  • Your settings are remembered in this browser; "Reset settings" puts them back.
  • Letter keys (M, S, T, W, A, B, C, D, V) reach the page when the map has focus. Elsewhere, in browse mode, your screen reader may use them itself (in NVDA, T jumps to a table and V to a visited link); switch to focus or forms mode, use the buttons, or use the access keys Alt + Shift + V (Terra) and Alt + Shift + W (Where am I) in Chrome or Edge on Windows.
  • When you move with the arrow keys, the tone follows every step at once. If you press quickly or hold the key, the announcement waits until you stop and then says where you ended up. While you keep moving, steps say only the value and the place; the map's name comes back after a pause.

The first question

Opened from a link, the site starts at the Gallery. There Terra introduces itself and asks how you would like to hear the site, then: "What would you like to explore?" 1 sea surface temperature, 2 air temperature, 3 vegetation, 4 precipitation, or 5 to look around the Gallery first. Answer with the number key, the buttons, or just say it ("vegetation", "number three"): Terra switches on the microphone at this point, and you can answer while it is still reading the choices. This happens once per visit.

On your first visit to any other page, before anything else, Terra, the assistant, introduces itself: what the site does, how to tell it what to do (press V, then talk, with examples), and how to explore a map yourself. Then it asks how you would like to hear the site: 1 built-in voice (Terra reads the site aloud), 2 I already use a screen reader, or 3 no voice, text only. A screen reader reads the introduction and the question. Without one, press any key or tap the screen to hear Terra say it: browsers only allow a page to speak after a key press or tap. If you choose the built-in voice straight away, Terra gives its introduction then. It is asked once; you can change the answer later under Voice in the Player, or by telling Terra. To hear it again, say "Terra, introduce yourself", or "start again" for the introduction and the question, or add ?welcome to the address.

Terra, the assistant

Instead of finding the right button, tell Terra what you want. Press V (or the "Terra listening" button, also in the Player's top bar) to switch Terra on: two rising notes, then "Terra is listening". Now just talk, whenever you like; Terra answers each request and keeps listening. Press V again, or say "stop listening", to switch it off (two falling notes). You can also type your request.

  • In the Player: "where am I", "take me to Japan", "what is the temperature in Spain", "go to the warmest", "next", "compare here with the coolest", "compare Brazil and Norway", "summary", "scan the equator", "move north a bit", "louder", "mute", "more detail", "tour", "demo", "what am I looking at".
  • On every page: "open the player", "open the rain map", "go to the gallery", "read this page" (then "continue"), "use the built-in voice", "repeat", "help", "stop listening".
  • Moving to another map, in many ways: "move me to the vegetation", "go to precipitation", "switch to air temperature", "open the sea temperature map", or just "rain". "What maps are there?" lists them.
  • Questions: "what is today's date?" and "what time is it?" (from your device's clock; the map's own data date is "what date is this map?"), "what is NDVI?" or "tell me about the rain map", "where does the data come from?", "introduce yourself", "how are you?". Terra is not a general AI: it is not connected to the internet, so it says so when asked about the weather, the news or anything outside this site.
  • Terra can ask you something back, for example after the summary: "Would you like me to take you to the warmest ocean area?" Answer yes or no.
  • When you talk to Terra, it answers out loud with the built-in voice, whatever the Voice setting, so you always hear it. A line under the switch shows what Terra heard ("Heard: …"). If you use a screen reader and would rather hear Terra through it, untick "Terra answers out loud".
  • Say its name any time: "Terra, are you still listening?" ("Yes, I'm listening."), "hey Terra, where am I?", or just "Terra" ("Yes? I'm listening."). Also "can you hear me?" and "are you there?".
  • Terra keeps listening while it talks, but ignores what it hears then unless it starts with "Terra" (which interrupts it). Usually, wait for it to finish, then speak.
  • In a busy room Terra hears people talking. It stays quiet unless it hears a request it knows (short phrases such as "where am I" or "next") or its name. Long sentences without "Terra" count as conversation. To ask anything else, start with "Terra".
  • In the Player, changing frame (the Frame menu, or "open the rain map") happens in the same page, so Terra keeps listening and keeps its voice.
  • Browsers only let a page speak after you have pressed a key, clicked or tapped on it. If Terra opens the Player from another page, it keeps listening, and a note asks you to press a key or tap once so it can answer out loud (a screen reader still hears it before that).
  • Terra stays on when it opens another page for you (for example "open the rain map"), and stays on until you switch it off or close the tab. The microphone is only used while Terra is on.
  • Terra does not answer itself: it pauses while the built-in voice is talking, and ignores what sounds like its own recent answers. A screen reader talking through speakers can still be heard by the microphone, so use headphones, or tick "Only answer when I start with Terra" and say, for example, "Terra, where am I?". Saying just "Terra" makes it answer "Yes?" and wait for your request.
  • Terra answers anything it hears that it does not understand with a short "Sorry, I did not catch …". If that happens often in a busy room, use the "Terra" wake word.
  • If you ask for a place where the map has no value (for example a country on the sea temperature map), Terra says so and gives the nearest value, and "go there" moves to it.
  • Terra is not a general AI and does not send your requests to an AI service. It understands a fixed set of requests, uses the same functions as the buttons, and only says values decoded from the map.
  • Speech recognition is done by your browser. In Chrome and Edge it uses an online service, so it needs an internet connection; Firefox does not offer it. Typing works everywhere, also offline.

If Terra does not answer

  1. Use Chrome, Edge or Safari, opened at the site's address (for example http://localhost:8931). A preview inside a code editor cannot use the microphone, and Firefox has no speech recognition.
  2. Press "Check the microphone" (or say "Terra, check the microphone"). It listens for 3 seconds, then asks you to speak once, and tells you: whether any sound reaches the page, and whether speech recognition turned it into words.
  3. "Sound: no" means the microphone is muted, the wrong one is chosen, or the browser has no permission (on a Mac: System Settings, Privacy and Security, Microphone, then allow your browser).
  4. Sound yes but no words: Chrome and Edge need an internet connection for speech recognition. Speak closer to the microphone.
  5. Read the "Microphone:" line under the switch. It shows where listening has got to: off, on and listening, hearing sound, hearing speech, heard you, or the exact problem (the microphone is blocked, no sound for 10 seconds, an error from Chrome, or a browser that cannot do speech recognition).
  6. Watch the line under the switch: "Heard: …" shows each phrase Terra caught and what it did with it (answered, stayed quiet, or ignored because it was talking).
  7. With people talking nearby, start with "Terra", speak close to the microphone, and pause for a moment after your request. Terra answers when your words stop, or about 3 seconds after its name if the room keeps talking. A headset microphone works best in a noisy room.

Touch

Drag a finger across the map to hear it. The page does not scroll while you drag on the map. The tone stops when you lift your finger. Tap the map five times quickly in one spot to hear where you are, including the nearest country.

With VoiceOver or TalkBack switched on, your screen reader takes the taps itself. Use the "Where am I?" button (double-tap it), ask Terra, or on iPhone use VoiceOver's Direct Touch (in the rotor) on the map.

On Android phones that support it, a short vibration marks very high values; iPhones do not support this, and nothing depends on it.

Headphones and volume

  • Headphones give the best result: left and right on the map are heard as left and right.
  • Start with your volume low. The Home page's Start audio button plays a short sound check.
  • The volume slider is capped below full volume, and a compressor on the output evens out sudden loud sounds.
  • Play the low, middle and high reference tones in the Sound legend before exploring, to tune your ear.

What has been tested

We have run automated tests in Chrome on macOS that use the Player with simulated keyboard and pointer input: arrow keys and Shift, the narration text matching the panel, mute, the volume limit, scan, reference tones, the "no data" sound, Summary, the Go to buttons, Compare (keys A, B and C), the Voice switch (only one voice at a time), detail levels, the edge announcement, Where am I, the tour, the volume keys, remembered settings, the sound cues, the display settings and the demo. We also check every page's structure: headings, landmarks, skip link, image text and control names. We have also checked the layout at phone width.

Not yet tested: real screen readers (VoiceOver, NVDA, JAWS, TalkBack), real phones, other browsers, and use by blind and low-vision people. This section will be updated with exactly what is tested, and with whom.
