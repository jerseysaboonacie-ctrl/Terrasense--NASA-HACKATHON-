How a colour becomes a sound

  1. The frame is a picture with a colour legend. NASA colours each point of the map by its value, and the legend says which colour means which value.
  2. The app finds the pixel under your pointer. It reads that pixel's colour straight from the image, which is stored on this site.
  3. It matches that colour to the closest legend colour. That gives an approximate value. If no legend colour is close enough, the app says "no data" instead of guessing.
  4. It turns the value into a pitch. Higher values play higher notes, on the same scale for every frame.

What this cannot tell you

  • Values are approximate. They are decoded from the colours of the visualization, not from the underlying measurements.
  • Image compression adds noise to the colours, so the app takes the middle colour of a small 3 by 3 pixel square.
  • Some colours look alike. On the air temperature frame, the coldest dark blue is closer to a mid-range purple-blue than we would like, so check surprising readings.
  • Land, water, logos and date labels are reported as "no data", never as a low value.
  • The vegetation frame's legend has no numbers, only Low and High, so its sound is relative.

To see every step for any point, use the decoder test page. The method and measurements are described in the README.
